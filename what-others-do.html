UGC Rights — what everyone else does

How the market handles content usage rights: what's bundled free, what costs extra, how it's priced, and where Benable's "buy the rights to this post" fits. Built from four parallel research tracks over official terms, help centers, and pricing pages, then an adversarial verification pass on every load-bearing claim.

4+2 research waves~150 primary sources fetched19 claims re-verified vs. primary docs58 UI screenshots archivedevery quote sourced · unverified marked
The one-line thesis

The market's loudest pitch is "no negotiation" — broad, perpetual rights bundled into the base price and marketed as a checkbox. Discrete, creator-paid rights purchases on already-live posts barely exist: TRIBE is the lone productized case (on its own campaign content), IZEA names it in a contract but prices nothing, Amazon caps it at 90 days of Amazon-only ads — and the biggest player in organic-UGC rights, Olapic/Social Native, pays creators $0 for perpetual all-channel consent. The genuinely unclaimed ground: visible creator payment at a flat pass-through price, on gifted-campaign content — and eventually any organic post. Keep the buying one-click, keep the negotiation invisible, pay the creator.

Corollary: Chiara's "I keep Social Cat for ad rights" almost certainly means she values their bundled-by-default simplicity — Social Cat has no rights store, it just includes everything. Matching that in future briefs (rights baked in up front, the Just Juice plan) and selling retroactive rights on past campaigns are two different products. This study covers both.
01 —

The landscape

Four camps, four different answers to "who owns the content?"

Gifted portals

Benable's direct comps
  • Social Cat · perpetual license, ads included, ToS-automatic
  • Statusphere · organic perpetual + ads 1yr, 1-click ad codes
  • minisocial · "mini license" perpetual + 30d whitelisting
  • Influenster/BV · perpetual for sampling, ads = separate consent
  • Skeepers · rights-for-product, creators waive extra comp
Their move: bundle everything, sell the absence of negotiation.

UGC studios

Made-to-order content
  • Billo · full IP assignment; creator-priced 30d ad add-ons
  • Insense · perpetual license; whitelisting $100–150/mo
  • JoinBrands · "unlimited rights" + free ad codes, all tiers
  • Cohley · client owns it, zero add-ons, zero platform cut
  • Twirl · "100% ownership" (ToS says license); £100/30d boosts
Their move: rights ride along with the content payment — never a separate line.

Enterprise + rails

Contracts & plumbing
  • #paid · named rights tiers + published volume discounts
  • Aspire / CreatorIQ · brokered ad-permission flows
  • Grin · freeform contracts, no rights tooling
  • TikTok Spark Ads · 7/30/60/365-day codes, revocable
  • Meta partnership ads · no expiry, revoke-anytime
Their move: authorization windows, not licenses — nothing portable off-platform.

Affiliate-adjacent

The surprise
  • ShopMy · quietly sells rights via "Amplify" (buy content from talent, white-labeled ads)
  • LTK · brand pages offer "commission, license, and distribute"; sublicense right in creator ToS
Their move: commissioning new content-plus-rights — not licensing already-live organic posts. The hypothesis that they "don't touch rights" is wrong; the narrower wedge is still open.

03 —

The receipts worth reading

Twelve platforms, verbatim, with the thing worth stealing from each. The last four came from the extension round that stress-tested the thesis.

Social Cat

the one Chiara pays for
The Creator grants the Brand a worldwide, perpetual, royalty-free, non-exclusive licence… This includes paid ads (organic, dark posts, and platform retargeting), the Brand's owned websites and apps, organic social, email, in-store and offline marketing.
thesocialcat.com/terms
No rights store, no toggle, no negotiation — the license is a fixed ToS term on every plan ($99–299/mo). Their comparison page shows "Licensed content included" as a ✓ for them and an ✗ for 10 named competitors. What they apparently lack: true Spark Ads / partnership-ads support (per third-party sources).
Steal: rights-as-checkbox marketing. Also the gap — they license broadly but can't run ads from the creator's handle. We can do both.

Statusphere

closest to Tony's vision
Say goodbye to chasing creators for usage rights… Every post auto-bundled with pre-negotiated usage & paid-media rights.
joinstatus.com/rights-ready-content
Contract splits organic (perpetual) from paid ads (1 year from first publish). Their AI "StevieOps" secures ad codes behind the scenes; brand clicks buy; and creators "set your ad rates to earn whenever brands boost your content." They name Insense's per-code fees as the villain in their sales copy.
Steal: the whole shape — pre-negotiated rates + one-click buy + creator-set pricing. This is the productized version of what Katie's doing manually.

#paid

only published price schema
3 months = 10% off · 6 months = 20% off · 12 months = 30% off
hashtagpaid.zendesk.com — Content Usage Rights
Named tiers: "Just Content" (~12-mo owned channels, default) → Creator Licensing (creator-handle ads) → Paid Social Rights (brand-handle ads) → Print/Broadcast/OOH extensions, priced per month with volume discounts. The only platform that publishes its rights-pricing logic.
Steal: the tier names map cleanly to how brands actually think. A future Benable rights menu could mirror this ladder.

Insense

the priced add-on model
Meta Partnership Ads: $100–150/month — TikTok Spark Ads: $100–150/month.
help.insense.pro/5141
Perpetual digital license vests automatically on approval + payment; whitelisting is a separate post-hire request flow, default 30 days when the brief is silent (confirmed in their ToS; the $100–150 figure comes from their help-center pricing chart). A G2 reviewer flags the dark side of blanket ToS consent: "creators are not aware of the platform terms (e.g. lifetime usage)."
Steal: the $100–150/mo whitelisting anchor. And the warning — invisible consent breeds resentment; our per-post opt-in SMS is the trust-friendly version.

Billo

creator-priced SKUs
Your right to use or display the Deliverables independently ends upon uploading them to the Billo Platform.
billo.app/terms-of-service-creators
Full IP assignment (rare — only Cohley matches it), then two à-la-carte creator-priced add-ons: organic posting (30 days) and partnership ads (Spark Ads is the TikTok format within it), price shown before purchase. Raw footage marketplace: creator sets price, Billo adds 25% for the brand, keeps 20%.
Steal: the raw-footage marketplace mechanics (creator price + transparent markup) — a clean model for a disclosed Benable take rate later.

Cohley

simplicity as the premium
No licensing fees, expiration dates, or surprises when you repurpose content months later.
cohley.com
Client owns everything, perpetual, "no hidden add-ons," and — their trust flex — "Cohley never takes a cut of creator compensation." Boosting codes are a workflow feature, not a SKU.
Steal: "no surprises months later" — the exact anxiety our brand email should pre-empt. And zero-cut-on-creator-pay as a stated trust signal (we already do this — say it).

ShopMy · Amplify

the disproven hypothesis
Purchase content directly from talent… for use on brand social channels or as white-labeled ads.
shopmy.us/home/brands/amplify
The belief that affiliate platforms don't touch rights is wrong — ShopMy sells them via a quietly-marketed sub-product, and LTK's brand pages offer "commission, license, and distribute." But both read as commissioning new content-plus-rights, not licensing already-live organic posts.
Steal: urgency. The adjacent platforms are already circling; the retroactive, already-posted wedge is still open — move while it is.

The rails

TikTok · Meta
Creators and other partners can revoke these permissions at any time.
facebook.com/business/help — Partnership ads
Spark Ads: creator-side codes in 7/30/60/365-day windows (brand-initiated requests default 365), revocable — a video must be un-authorized before it can even be deleted. Meta: no expiry, revoke-anytime. Critically, neither grants anything portable — no raw file, no website/email use, nothing off-platform. Ad codes authorize boosting; they are not licenses.
Steal: the framing for brands — "an ad code is not a license." The portable, off-platform, raw-file license is precisely the thing only a deal like ours delivers.

TRIBE

the thesis-bender
You must not use a Post in any form of paid, sponsored or promoted advertising.
tribegroup.co/toc — the default grant
The one platform that productized our exact transaction: brands can "request to license APPROVED and PUBLISHED content" — the creator gets an in-app notification, sees the price and terms, and accepts or declines per piece. The license picker is a clean 2×2: online-only vs all-channels × 12-month vs perpetual, plus separate "Ad Boosting Rights" (7–365 days). Scope limit: it only works on TRIBE's own campaign content, not independent organic posts.
Steal: the 2×2 picker with a visible total before the creator is asked — it turns "buy the rights" into a two-question form, and the accept/decline screen doubles as informed consent.

IZEA Flex

the named category
…license to… distribute pre-existing content provided by the Creator solely on the Distribution Channels and for the Content Usage Period.
izea.com/flex-tos — "Organic Content"
The only contract in the study that names a paid license category for content that existed before the deal — alongside Licensed, Owned, and Sponsored Social, each with a plain-English sentence before the legal grant. But: every term is negotiated per offer, nothing is priced, and there's no self-serve checkout.
Steal: the four-way taxonomy. "Organic Content" is the precise name for what Benable is selling — nobody else in the study even names it.

Amazon SCR

capped + extendable
The content license is valid for 90 days. To continue using the content in ads after that period, you can extend the license by paying the pre-set rate.
Amazon Creator Connections — SCR Brand Guide
A genuine discrete, creator-compensated rights purchase (~$100–200+/asset, flat, creator-set) — but brief-first and locked to Amazon's own ad units, nothing off-Amazon. The creator-protective detail: the fee is owed once the creator accepts, whether or not the brand uses the content.
Steal: the mechanic, not the trigger — capped license + pay-a-preset-rate-to-extend, and fee-owed-on-accept.

Olapic / Social Native

the cautionary giant
FatFace will not pay you for the use of your User Content and/or for any intellectual property rights connected with them.
tos.olapic.com/fatface — live today
The biggest machine in organic-UGC rights runs on unpaid consent: reply #YESFATFACE / #YESBIRKENSTOCK and the brand gets a perpetual, irrevocable, all-channel license — ads included — for nothing. Their modern marketplace pays creators well ($3,200 briefs), but only for commissioned content; rights on organic posts still cost brands $0 and pay creators $0.
Steal: their transparency language ("license terms documented on every asset") — paired with the thing they don't do: actually paying. That's Benable's trust wedge in one sentence.

05 —

So is $50–100 flat fair?

The market has no price for our exact transaction — which cuts in our favor, with one caveat.

No published framework prices "rights-only, after the fact, on already-posted gifted content." Every % above assumes a cash base fee that gifted campaigns don't have. The honest zero-point for a gift is $0 — per the Creators Guild of America, a true gift obligates the creator to nothing, so any cash offered afterward is an upgrade on the norm, not a discount from it.

Anchors: a nano creator's entire new paid post runs $50–300 all-in (Later's benchmarks); UGC flat licensing runs ~$150–300/video; and the 2–5× perpetuity multiplier applied to a notional nano base implies $100–1,500 — but that heuristic was built for paid productions with exclusivity risk, which this isn't.

Verdict: $50–100 is fair-to-generous for nano and lower-micro creators (the bulk of our roster) — as long as the grant stays narrow-ish and we frame it as "cash on top of the product you already received." The caveat: our bundle includes raw files + paid ads + perpetuity, which the market prices as stacking premiums. For a savvier 25–50K creator, expect counters in the $150–500 range — which is exactly why the email now promises "we'll negotiate and do our best" instead of a fixed outcome, and why the SMS decline reply asks for their number.

06 —

What this changes

For Friday's manual test

Small edits, better armor

  • The negotiation close was the right call. Creators may counter above band — the market says $150–500 is a plausible ask for what we're bundling. Best-effort language keeps every promise keepable.
  • Lead with what an ad code can't do. Chiara can get Spark codes elsewhere; only a license gets her the raw file, her website, her emails, forever. That's the differentiated half of our pitch.
  • Say the pass-through out loud. Cohley markets "we never take a cut of creator compensation" as a trust feature. We already do this — it belongs in the email ("the full amount goes to the creator").
  • Raw file = real value. Sources price it at +20–100%. If a creator balks, offering "posted version only, lower price" is a legitimate fallback tier, not a failure.
  • Pre-empt "surprises months later." The plain-English license recap we send after purchase is exactly the anxiety-killer the market messaging targets. Keep it.
For the product

The whitespace, mapped

  • The wedge is real, but claim it precisely. TRIBE productized post-hoc licensing (2×2 picker, creator accepts a visible price) on its own campaign content; IZEA names "Organic Content" licensing but prices nothing; Amazon caps it at 90 days of Amazon-only ads. Still unclaimed: flat-price, pass-through, creator-paid rights on gifted-campaign content — and truly independent organic posts, which today only unpaid Olapic-style consent-harvesting touches.
  • Two products, not one: rights-in-the-brief (match Social Cat's bundled parity — the Just Juice play) and the retroactive rights store (the differentiator). Don't let the second get built as a discount version of the first.
  • Duration menu should mirror the rails: 30 days (the whitelisting default everywhere), 1 year (Statusphere's paid-ads term, Spark's max code), perpetual (the license the rails can't grant). Three tiers, done.
  • Creator-set rates with a floor — creators told us they want auto-negotiation with a minimum (Sophia's call, April). Statusphere proves the mechanic ships.
  • Disclose the take rate when we add one. Nobody in the market prices a rights-specific cut cleanly; Billo's raw-footage marketplace (creator price + transparent markup) is the model worth copying.
  • Revocability is table stakes on the rails — every native mechanism lets creators pull access anytime. A perpetual license is our differentiator, but likeness-based takedown paths (Twirl's 12-month right, Skeepers' 20-year cap) are the humane precedents to study before productizing.
07 —

The screens themselves

What brands and creators actually see, reconstructed from help-center walkthroughs and product docs — 58 screenshots archived locally in ui-shots/ so they survive link rot. Every chip below is verbatim on-screen text. The pattern across all 25 platforms: rights UI concentrates at four moments.

1 · The brief Rights terms set before content exists — Aspire's duration dropdown, IZEA's usage-period picker, Insense's campaign-goal fork.
2 · The checkout Rights as a line item at purchase — Billo's tier table, Collabstr's usage-rights field, TRIBE's 2×2 picker.
3 · The approval Clicking "approve" IS the rights transfer — soona says it outright; Social Cat does it silently on upload.
4 · The handoff Ad permissions finish on Meta/TikTok's own screens — every platform eventually ejects to a native consent sheet.

TRIBE

the reference flow — closest to what we'd build

Brand sees

  1. On any approved & published post: a License Content button.
  2. The 2×2 picker — online or all channels × 12 months or perpetual.
  3. Price + specs check before committing: "Check the total price, and ensure the resolution and specs suit your needs."
  4. Ads are a separate flow: click Boost, pick 7/30/60/365 days, request goes to the creator.

Creator sees

  1. Push notification: "A brand has requested to license your content."
  2. In-app: POSTS → Requested tab under Content Rights — the request card shows a fixed, non-negotiable price.
  3. Prompted to read the Content Rights Terms — told the post stays on their profile but can't be reused elsewhere once licensed.
  4. Two buttons: Decline Accept — accepting starts payout (48h or 45d).

The brief-builders

IZEA · Aspire · GRIN · Later · Collabstr · Social Cat — rights as form fields

Brand sees

  1. IZEA Flex: Content Usage Terms picker — 12, 24, 36 months, perpetual, or custom, 12 months is the system default — plus a Paid Media Yes/No toggle (default Yes) and an Exclusivity Period field.
  2. Aspire: Standard Brief has a usage-duration dropdown (3 months, 6 months, 1 year, or Custom) and an Ad Rights toggle; the content library filters by content usage rights.
  3. GRIN: Authorization Period for TikTok Spark Ads + an after-the-fact Ad authorization request.
  4. Later: no structured field at all — rights language typed into a free-text brief; Partnership Ads tracked per-post (Request → Add to Ads Manager / Permission Denied).
  5. Collabstr: a Usage rights free-text field at checkout, in escrow until the creator accepts.
  6. Social Cat: a contract modal (type, fee, deadline, deliverables) — but no rights language anywhere in the flow; downloads are just Save As.

Creator sees

  1. IZEA: offer-review page with a terms section covering payment + content usage, then Accept Offer / Negotiate / Decline Offer — the only brief-builder with a built-in counter path.
  2. GRIN: a mandatory Content Rights step in the proposal wizard — terms must be accepted to proceed.
  3. Collabstr: order card with Content Requirements (usage rights inside) and 72 hours to accept.
  4. Social Cat: nothing — creators learn brands get usage rights from a help-center FAQ, and uploading content silently transfers the license.
Where the rights UI is the absence of UI: minisocial (rights live in the signed MSA — content just appears as downloads), Stack Influence ("full usage rights" is a marketing line next to a download button; whitelisting codes auto-requested behind the scenes), Kale (the license is granted at account creation — the challenge flow never mentions it), Upfluence (contracts are uploaded PDFs; whitelisting walkthroughs point at Meta's native screens), and LTK (no rights surface found anywhere in LTK Connect's documentation trail). For these platforms the entire rights experience is a clause the creator agreed to before any specific post existed — which is exactly the opacity a visible, per-post, paid consent screen can beat.
