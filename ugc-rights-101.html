Types of Content Usage Rights

When a creator makes content for or about a brand, the content itself and the right to use it are two separate things. This is the most important concept in UGC contracts. Here's every type of right you'll encounter.

Included Organic Usage Rights

What it is: The brand reposts creator content on their own social channels, website, or landing pages — without paying to boost or advertise it.

  • Usually included automatically for smaller creators (nano/micro tier)
  • Standard window: 30 days from post date included in base fee
  • Beyond 30 days, brands pay ~10% of base fee per additional month
  • Covers: brand social feeds, website embeds, email newsletters
"Organic is something usually another line item, but it typically is included by the creator. Unless you're working with someone like Alex Earle, you would have to probably pay."
— Hanah Bae, Creator Call (Mar 25, 2026)

Add-on Paid Usage Rights

What it is: The brand runs paid advertisements using the creator's content — Meta Ads, Google Ads, TikTok Ads, boosted posts. This is never included in the base fee.

  • Charged as a monthly add-on: typically +20% of base fee per month
  • Must be negotiated separately from content creation fee
  • Duration matters: 30-day, 90-day, 6-month, or 12-month terms
  • This is the #1 thing brands want right now — content for ads
"UGC, if like this included paid usage, I think would be like a really good fit. We're trying to get more like UGC paid ads."
— Amanda Morris, Solara SunCare (Mar 25, 2026)
"Oh, I love when I post an organic video and a brand recognizes it and then they give — they pay for that video for usage rights. So is that what you're about?"
— Hanah Bae, Creator Call (Mar 25, 2026)

Premium Whitelisting / Dark Posting

What it is: The brand runs paid ads from the creator's actual account, so the ads look like native creator posts — not brand ads. "Dark posting" means the ad doesn't appear on the creator's feed.

  • Higher premium than standard paid ads: +30% of base fee per month
  • Requires the creator to grant advertising access to their account
  • Significantly higher CTR and trust than regular brand ads
  • Contract must specify: level of access, duration, creative approval rights
  • On TikTok, this is done through "Spark Ads"
"There is. And some brands in Instagram will actually request UGC so I don't have to post on my page. Yep. They would use my account for dark listing [dark posting]."
— Hanah Bae, Creator Call (Mar 25, 2026)
!

Key risk: Account access confusion is a leading cause of contract disputes. Contracts should specify "advertising permission only" vs "full admin access" — and always include a clear end date.

Negotiated Exclusivity Clauses

What it is: Restrictions preventing the creator from working with competing brands during (and sometimes after) the campaign period.

  • Category exclusivity: Can't work with any brand in the same category (e.g., all sunscreen)
  • Direct competitor: Only specific named brands are restricted
  • Geographic: Restricted in certain regions only
  • Typical premium: +10-25% of base fee
  • Common timeframes: 30 days (standard), 90 days (mid), 6 months (rare)
"Would there be another competitive sunscreen in the bag?" — Her founder Stephanie will ask this. Maria handled well: first come first served, wouldn't put them with a direct competitor.
— Tony Staehelin on Solara SunCare call (Mar 25, 2026)

Perpetual Rights (Forever)

Brand can use the content indefinitely, with no renewal. Costs 100-300% of base fee. Generally unfavorable for creators — eliminates future licensing income. Increasingly rare as creators become more business-savvy.

Time-Limited Rights (Standard)

Usage rights expire after a set period (30, 90, 180 days, or 12 months). Industry standard. Each extension adds cost incrementally. Better for both parties — brands pay for what they use, creators maintain future value.

Full Buyout (Work-for-Hire)

Brand owns ALL intellectual property and copyright. Creator can't reuse the content even in their own portfolio. Priced at 200-300%+ of base fee. Used when brands want exclusive, proprietary content.

Limited License (Standard)

Creator retains copyright; brand gets defined usage rights only. Creator can still use content in their portfolio, reel, and case studies. Much more common and creator-friendly.

How Contracts Are Structured

Creator contracts separate the content creation fee from usage rights add-ons. Here's what goes where.

Total = Base Fee + Usage Add-ons + Exclusivity Premium + Bonuses
The industry-standard compensation formula for creator campaigns

Included in Base Fee

Content creation (1-3 deliverables), initial organic usage rights (30 days), one round of revisions, platform licensing within agreed scope.

NOT Included (Costs Extra)

Paid/boosted ad usage, whitelisting, extended organic reuse, exclusivity, full buyout, rush fees, raw footage, additional cuts, rights on new platforms.

"So there's not an additional fee on top of that? The content is included in that fee?" — She's clearly been burned by platforms that layer costs.

Three Pricing Models

Most Common Flat Fee

Fixed price for defined deliverables. Example: $2,000 for Instagram post + 30-day organic rights. Clean, predictable, easy to contract.

Growing Trend Hybrid (Base + Performance)

Base fee plus bonuses tied to results. Example: "$3,000 base + $500 bonus per 50K impressions above 200K." Aligns creator and brand incentives.

Emerging Performance-Only

Commission or CPA only, no base fee. Example: 5% commission on attributed sales. Common in affiliate/e-commerce campaigns. ~20-30% of major campaigns now use this model.

Glossary

Quick-reference definitions for every term you'll hear in UGC rights conversations. Click to expand.

Rights & Licensing

Legal permission to use creator content in specific ways. The creator retains copyright unless explicitly transferred via buyout. Usage rights define where, how, and for how long the brand can use the content.

Brand reposting creator content on their own channels (social feeds, website, email) without paid promotion. Standard: 30 days included in base fee. Extension costs ~10% per additional month.

Brand running paid advertisements (Meta Ads, Google Ads, TikTok Ads) using creator content. Never included in base fee. Typically +20% of base per month.

Brand receives advertising access to creator's account and runs paid ads from that account. Ads look like native creator posts, significantly boosting trust and CTR. Premium pricing: +30% of base per month. On TikTok, done via Spark Ads.

Brand runs ads using creator's content/likeness, but the ad doesn't appear on the creator's actual feed or profile. It only shows to the targeted ad audience. Lower trust than true whitelisting but still premium-priced.

Brand purchases full intellectual property and copyright ownership. Creator cannot reuse the content in any form. Priced at 200-300%+ of base fee. Rare for UGC; more common in traditional advertising.

Creator retains copyright; brand gets defined usage rights only for specific platforms, durations, and use cases. Creator can still use content in portfolio. Industry standard for UGC/influencer campaigns.

Agreement preventing creator from working with competing brands during a specified period. Types: category exclusivity (same industry), direct competitor (specific brands), geographic (regional). Costs the creator lost opportunities, so brands typically pay a 10-25% premium.

Creator Types

UGC Creator: Produces high-quality content specifically for brands to use on brand channels. Value is content quality, not audience size. No large personal following required.

Influencer: Has an established personal following (typically 10K+) who recommends products to their audience. Value is audience reach and trust.

Many creators are both — they produce content on their own channels AND license that content to brands.

  • Nano (1K-10K): High niche authority, cost-effective, great for UGC
  • Micro (10K-100K): Strong engagement relative to reach, ROI-efficient
  • Mid-Tier (100K-500K): Professional quality, broader reach
  • Macro (500K-1M): Major reach, significant investment
  • Mega (1M+): Celebrity-level, highest cost

Business & Campaign Terms

The specific pieces of content a creator must produce, spelled out in the contract. Example: "2 TikTok videos, 1 Instagram Reel, 3 static images." Revisions are usually limited (1-2 rounds included; extra rounds cost $100-$500 each).

Formula: (Likes + Comments + Shares) / Total Followers x 100. The key metric for judging influencer value. In 2026, above 3% is considered high, above 5% is exceptional. More important than raw follower count for campaign ROI.
